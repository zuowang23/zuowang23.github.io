How to Be a Good Theoretical Physicist in the Age of AI

Zuo Wang · zuowang23.github.io · Last updated November 2026

Gerard ’t Hooft’s page How to become a GOOD Theoretical Physicist lays out what to learn, from mathematics up through quantum field theory. Every item there still applies. This page is about something different: how to work, now that AI can derive, compute and even prove things for us.

In 2026 an AI system produced a claimed proof for one of the Millennium Prize Problems, and AI tools are now part of daily work in theoretical physics. I am an early-career theorist, and I wrote this list for myself, to keep in mind which skills will keep a physicist valuable as these tools improve. I share it in case it helps others too. It is opinionated, incomplete, and will change as AI changes.

The guiding principle

AI is making answers cheap. What stays scarce is knowing which questions matter, understanding what the answers mean, and the judgment to tell when they are wrong. Build those three things above everything else.

How to use this list. Don’t try to do everything at once. Each semester, pick three unticked items and work on them deliberately. The rhythm at the end suggests what to repeat weekly, monthly and yearly. Your ticks are saved in your own browser.

1Foundations still matter

You cannot judge what you cannot do yourself. As AI writes more of our derivations, solid fundamentals turn from a requirement into a competitive advantage.

2Research taste

Choosing problems is the skill AI takes over last. Train it deliberately.

3Working with AI

Treat AI like lab equipment: master it, calibrate it, and never trust it blindly.

4Verification

This is the physicist’s version of formal proof. It becomes more important the more you rely on machines.

5Staying close to nature

Physics answers to experiment. A theorist connected to real systems has leverage that symbolic manipulation alone does not.

6Building a research program

Committees hire a direction, not a pile of papers. Make your work add up to a story.

13A rhythm for using this list

A checklist only helps if you come back to it. This is the rhythm I aim for.

Every week
Read one experimental paper. Spend an hour thinking with paper and pencil, without AI. Run the verification checks on whatever I produced that week.
After every talk or poster
Log the title, venue, date and link. Write down the question I couldn’t answer well, and answer it.
Every month
Review the problem notebook. Check which project is closest to finished, and finish it.
Every semester
Pick three unticked items from this page to work on. Update the homepage.
Every year
Re-derive the core results of my field by hand. Rewrite the research statement and refresh the CV. Reread this page and change what no longer fits.
